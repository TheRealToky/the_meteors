**"How did you check your numbers?"** Answer in three steps:

1. **Extraction script.** We exported each NISR table to CSV, and `pipeline/extract.py` turns them into JSON. It **fails** if a total doesn't match the report (e.g. the population must equal 14,108,217, and grooms and brides must each sum to 50,256).
2. **Validation script.** `pipeline/validate.py` re-checks the totals and confirms every text exists in all three languages with matching placeholders.
3. **Manual pass.** We did an on-screen number verification playtest (31 Oct), comparing each displayed number with the report.

**Data conflicts we found and resolved yourselves** (judges love this):

- Deaths are 39,378 in sheet 10.2 but 39,355 in Table 12. We used **Table 12**, the detailed mortality table.
- Figure 19 jumps from 75–79 to "85+" with no 80–84 row, yet the totals add up. We labelled the last group **80+**.

## 4. Design choices (Clarity): why each chart, colour and layout

| Element                    | Choice                                                                                 | Why                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Overall format             | Walk along a path; one station per life event                                          | Follows the report's own structure (birth → marriage → divorce → death) and turns tables into a story.                     |
| Population                 | **Pyramid** (age × sex)                                                                | The standard, instantly recognised way to show age–sex structure; it makes Rwanda's youthfulness obvious.                  |
| Births                     | **One dot per registered birth** (~357k dots), with _you_ highlighted                  | Makes the size of the number felt, not just read. It also reads as "1 in 356,838".                                         |
| Marriage / divorce / death | **Guess first, then a bar chart**                                                      | Guessing makes people commit to an expectation, so the real data surprises them and sticks. Bars suit counts by age group. |
| Marriage trend             | **Line chart** 2019–2025                                                               | A trend over time; the COVID dip in 2020 is visible at a glance.                                                           |
| Divorce                    | Bars split into **granted in 2025 / granted earlier**                                  | Shows on screen why divorces ÷ marriages is not a rate.                                                                    |
| Causes of death            | **Two separate ranked lists** (facility vs. community), not one pie                    | They come from different diagnostic systems and shouldn't be merged. Twenty categories would also be unreadable as a pie.  |
| Colours                    | Blue = male, green = female, orange/yellow = _your guess_                              | Neutral sex colours (not pink/blue). The highlight colour is reserved for the viewer, so "you" are always findable.        |
| Layout                     | Large chart card in the centre; source label top-right; caption bottom-left            | Each screen answers what it is, where it comes from, and what to take away.                                                |
| Accessibility              | "View as table" on every chart, keyboard controls, screen-reader text, three languages | Inclusive, and it lets judges check exact values.                                                                          |
| Atmosphere                 | Soft 3D hills, calm piano, sky that moves from dawn to night as you age                | Emotional framing, especially for death, which is presented respectfully as "The Final Horizon".                           |

**What you left out on purpose:**

- **No divorce "rate"** computed from divorces ÷ marriages (different cohorts).
- **No "risk of dying" or life expectancy** from registered deaths, because only half of deaths are registered.
- **No merged cause-of-death ranking**, for the reason above.
- **Not every report table.** District breakdowns (Tables 14, 10.10) and some vital rates were extracted but left out to keep one clear story.

## 5. Limitations: raise them before the judges do

1. **Registered ≠ occurring.** Death data covers about half of deaths, and that coverage is uneven: 57.6% for males vs 43.6% for females. So the 127.4 male:female death ratio partly reflects _who gets registered_, not only who dies.
2. **A composite life.** The "life" combines one year (2025) with 2019–2025 trends; it is not one real cohort followed over time. We state this in the intro and the methodology panel.
3. **Projections.** The 2025 population figures are projections from the 2022 census, not counts.
4. **Community causes of death** come from verbal autopsy and are less precise (e.g. "unspecified cardiac diseases").
5. **The Youth station has no statistic.** It's a pacing moment. With more time we'd add one, e.g. 57% of the population is under 25.
6. **Divorce timing.** 41.3% of 2025 divorce registrations relate to divorces granted earlier, so durations mix several cohorts.
7. **Device needs.** It's a 3D app (~55 MB of models and music), so it needs a reasonably modern device; slow connections will struggle.
